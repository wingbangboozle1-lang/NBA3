Seven gates, the statistics behind them, and how every candidate scored.

The seven validation gates

A strategy must clear all seven before it is eligible for the leaderboard. The thresholds are proposed, not approved — see question Q-07 and suggestion SG-06.

Statistics used, and why

  • Exact binomial test. Used instead of a normal approximation because several candidate samples are small (n = 11 and n = 17), where the normal approximation is invalid.
  • Benjamini–Hochberg at 0.05 across the eight-strategy family, so that testing eight candidates does not manufacture a false positive.
  • Pearson r with a Fisher z interval. Every correlation is reported with a 95% confidence interval. An interval containing zero is described as inconclusive, never as a point value.
  • Student-t for bias and for paired squared-error comparisons against the naive baseline, implemented from the incomplete beta function (no scipy available).
  • OLS with a sum-to-zero constraint row to make the Massey rating system identifiable.

All of these are implemented from first principles in model/nbastats.py; exact-binomial bounds follow the method set out in the NIST e-Handbook, section 7.2.7.1.

What the gates cannot fix

  • No market price. G1–G7 test self-consistency against our own line. They cannot test profitability, because no odds are held (F-03).
  • One season, 114 out-of-sample games. No regime test, no multi-season test, no ablation is possible yet.
  • Selection bias risk. The eight rules were chosen by us, on this sample. A pre-registered rule set and a frozen model register (SG-12) are needed before any of this is treated as confirmatory.

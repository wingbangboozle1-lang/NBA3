The two provisional signals, honestly

S5 — fade totals of 235+ 21 of 24 out of sample, p = 0.0003. Survives Benjamini–Hochberg at 0.05 and is split-half stable. But 80% power at the observed effect needs n ≥ 56, and the sample gate requires 100. It is also built on the totals model, which is worse than its baseline out of sample — so this is more likely a bias in the estimator than an edge.
S4b — back road favourites of 8+ 20 of 28 out of sample, p = 0.036. Does not survive Benjamini–Hochberg at 0.05 across the eight-strategy family, and needs n ≥ 171 for 80% power. Eight candidates were tested, so one p-value near 0.04 is about what chance produces.

Removing luck, in practice

  • G3 hard floor of 100 bets before anything can be called deployable.
  • G4 prints the sample needed for 80% power at the observed effect. S1 would need 102,005 bets; S3 would need 9,074. That is what "no evidence" looks like when stated honestly.
  • G6 Benjamini–Hochberg at 0.05 across the whole family, so testing eight candidates does not manufacture a winner.
  • G7 split-half direction stability, which S3 and S5b both fail.
  • G1/G2 provenance and no-look-ahead are structural: every input is an official NBA page, and every prediction uses only games that finished earlier.

What is not yet in place: a proper-scoring-rule primary metric (Brier / log loss) and calibration reporting. That is suggestion SG-06, awaiting a decision.

Methodology

The engines, the walk-forward design, and the statistics behind the gates.

How a line is originated

Two margin engines and two totals engines are fitted on completed games only, then selected on out-of-sample RMSE. The primary published line is the model that won the walk-forward comparison; the efficiency-based estimate is shown beside it as a cross-check only, because it uses season-to-date ratings and putting it in a backtest would leak future games.

  1. LM-A additive. Each team's points for and points against per game, league-centred, combined into a margin.
  2. LM-B Massey. Least-squares net ratings plus a home-court constant, fitted by OLS with a sum-to-zero constraint row to make the system identifiable.
  3. LM-C blend. The mean of A and B margins, with the T2 totals estimator.
  4. Totals T1. Points-for plus points-against, league-centred.
  5. Totals T2. The sum of the two teams' own scoring rates.

A team must have at least 3 completed games before it is rated; the first 60 games are burn-in and produce no out-of-sample prediction.

Removing luck

Luck cannot be removed from a single outcome. It is controlled in the evaluation:

  • Expanding-window walk-forward with a 60-game burn-in; no future game ever enters a fit.
  • Exact binomial test against the correct null. For the two-leg parlay the null is the joint rate implied by the observed leg rates under independence (0.219), not 0.50 — testing a parlay against 50% would be a category error.
  • Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control at 0.05 across the eight-strategy family.
  • Split-half stability: the direction of the effect must hold in both halves of the sample.
  • A hard minimum sample of 100 bets before anything can be called deployable.
  • Required n for 80% power at the observed effect size, printed next to every result.
  • Proper scoring and calibration are the recommended primary metric, not raw win rate (see suggestion SG-06).

What the numbers say

  • Home points vs away points, same game: r = 0.128 over 174 games. A game total is mildly correlated with how one-sided it is, but the effect is small.
  • Spread residual vs total residual: r = 0.091, 95% CI -0.094 to 0.270, n = 114 out-of-sample. The interval contains zero, so dependence between a side and a total in the same game is not established at this sample size.
  • Joint parlay hit rate 0.246 against 0.219 under independence. The two are close; a two-leg same-game parlay built on our own spread and total behaves, on this sample, about as independence would predict. That is a statement about our lines, not about any market price.
  • Cross-game parlays are marked unverified. Independence across different games is an assumption, not a measured result, and no interval is available.

What is blocked, and by what

Three rows are blank for the same reason: this project holds season-to-date player averages, not per-player game logs. Same-team player-player correlations, and within-player stat combinations, cannot be computed from averages alone. Capturing nba.com/stats/players/boxscores by date would unblock them — see question Q-06 and suggestion SG-03.

Do not multiply marginals. Until leg dependence is measured with an interval, any same-game parlay price derived by multiplying independent probabilities is an assumption presented as a number.

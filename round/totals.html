Totals: possessions × points-per-possession

The total is modelled the way a basketball game actually produces points: a number of possessions times a number of points per possession. The rejected alternative — summing the two teams' scoring averages — is printed beside it so the difference is visible rather than hidden.

Possessions are the mean of the two teams' measured PACE (a per-game possession count on the official advanced page). Points per 100 possessions are built from season points over season possessions, which the build checks against the page's own OffRtg.

Why this and not the average of averages

Summing two scoring averages silently assumes both teams score at their own season rate against this opponent and at this pace. It cannot respond to a slow team meeting a fast one, and it cannot respond to a good offence meeting a good defence. The possessions form can, because the possession count comes from the two teams' measured pace and the points-per-possession comes from each side's own measured efficiency.

The build checks the unit chain in three places before it prints anything: POSS/GP must reproduce the page's PACE; PTS·GP/POSS·100 must reproduce the page's OffRtg; and the resulting total must sit inside a plausible 190–260 band. All three are unit tests, not prose.

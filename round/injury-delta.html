Why the obvious diff is the wrong diff

Comparing the whole 11/15 filing with the whole 11/16 filing looks like the natural thing to do, and it produces a dramatic-looking table: hundreds of players "added" and "removed". Almost none of it is injury news. The two documents describe different basketball games, so a player who is not playing on 11/15 simply disappears and a player who is playing on 11/16 simply appears. For the record, that comparison on this pair yields 57 rows present only in the newer report and 84 only in the earlier one — slate turnover, not status movement. It is printed here once, labelled, and not used for anything.

A defect that two official documents agree on

Jimmy Butler III has no printed status in either filing. Both the 12:00 AM ET and the 09:15 AM ET reports print six cells for his row where the header promises seven: the Current Status cell is absent and the reason text sits where the status belongs. Because both documents show the same defect for the same player, it is a property of the league's printer rather than of this project's extraction. The row is published with a blank status — it is not inferred, and it is not copied from an earlier day's report — and it appears on the injuries page and on flagged.

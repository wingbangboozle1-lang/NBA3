Player Props

Point projections from official season averages — and a clear statement of why they are not yet bettable numbers.

What these projections are, and are not

The season averages on this table are official, taken from nba.com/stats/players/traditional. Everything in a Projected column is ours, and it rests on an assumption we want to be blunt about:

Assumption (unverified): the minutes vacated by injured players are redistributed to the remaining rotation players in proportion to their season minutes. We have no per-player game logs with which to test this, no usage-rate data, and no distribution for any individual game. The projection is a point estimate of a mean, not a distribution, so it cannot tell you the probability of going over a prop line.

That means we will not publish an over/under call on a player prop from this data. The table below shows the projection and the season baseline side by side so a reviewer can see exactly how much the assumption moves the number — in most cases, very little, because the captured minutes set only covers the league's heaviest-used players and the vacuum is spread thinly across them.

What it would take to make these tradeable

  1. Per-player game logs. Without them there is no standard deviation, and without a standard deviation there is no probability of exceeding a line. This is the blocking item.
  2. Usage rate and minutes share when a teammate is out. The pro-rata assumption is almost certainly wrong in detail: stars absorb far more of a vacuum than bench players do.
  3. Official prop lines to compare against. We deliberately do not scrape sportsbook prices; if that changes, the comparison becomes possible.

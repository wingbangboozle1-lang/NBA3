Questions that must be answered before any decision

This is the register the brief asked for: plain questions, what we can already answer from evidence in hand, what is genuinely missing, and the concrete step that would close each gap. Nothing here is answered by assumption.

How to read the "known" column. It states only what this repository or a linked source actually establishes. "Not provided" means exactly that — it is not a placeholder for a sensible default, and no default has been substituted.

The three that block everything else

  1. Q3 — no closing-line history. Without it, five of six strategies cannot be scored. This is not a modelling gap, it is a data acquisition gap, and no amount of code closes it.
  2. Q1 — legal and licensed use. Model outputs are one thing; publishing them as actionable picks is another. This determines what the site is allowed to be.
  3. Q9 — no minutes source. Props and same-game combos are the largest market by volume and are entirely out of reach until this is solved.

What we can already answer with confidence

  • The coming slate. 23 games across three days, each with an official game ID, tip time and venue, all cross-checked between two sources.
  • Who is unavailable. 75 named statuses from the league's own 5 p.m. report, with the reason for each.
  • What the market thinks. Nine full two-way prices with open and current numbers, de-vigged.
  • How noisy a result has to be before it means anything. Computed explicitly: roughly 778 bets to establish a 5% edge.

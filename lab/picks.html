Signals, screening and picks

The model produces a probability for every market on every priced game. A raw probability difference is not a pick: it has to clear a screen that asks whether the disagreement is defensible at all. Here is the whole funnel, including the rejections.

Why most signals are thrown away. A model that ignores injuries will disagree with a market that prices them, and every disagreement looks like edge. The screens exist to throw most of those disagreements away rather than to package them as picks.

The screening rules, stated before the results

  1. De-vig first. No comparison is made against a raw implied probability. The two-way price has the book's margin removed, so a 4.5% hold cannot masquerade as edge.
  2. Divergence cap. A modelled spread more than 4 points from the listed spread (or a total more than 5 points away) is rejected. With no injury adjustment, a gap that wide is far more likely to be our blind spot than the market's.
  3. Injury exposure cap. If the official report lists any player Out on either side beyond a threshold, the signal is rejected outright. We cannot defend a probability we know is computed from a stale roster.
  4. Value is EV, not edge. A probability edge with no price advantage is not a bet. Ranked by expected value per unit staked at the listed price.

The one signal that survived

OKC@LAC — Total Under 222.5 at -112. Model 55.5% versus de-vigged market 50.4%, expected value +0.051 per unit. Both sides of this game were clean on the official report at the time of the snapshot.

It survived screening. That is not the same as being validated: it has not been through a single validation gate. Treat it as a hypothesis with a number attached.

"Edge (pp)" is model probability minus de-vigged market probability, in percentage points. "EV / unit" is the expected profit per 1 unit staked at the listed American price: p x (decimal - 1) - (1 - p). Divergence is the absolute gap between the modelled and listed number for that market, in points.

Combination bets. No parlay or same-game parlay is offered as a pick. A parlay needs leg prices for the combined product, and none were captured. What was measured is the dependence between legs — see Player props & correlation — which is the part most parlay content skips entirely.

Injuries, rotation minutes and game flow

The league publishes a timestamped availability report, not a minutes forecast. This page keeps that distinction explicit: the status is official and quoted exactly, while every attempt to turn a status into points or minutes is labelled as either derived or impossible.

The reporting rule, from the league itself. Teams must report a participation status and a specific reason by 5 p.m. local time the day before a game (1 p.m. on the day for the second of a back-to-back), and must file a game-day update between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. Reports are revised continuously. So a status is a snapshot with an expiry, and the timestamp is part of the data. NBA Official — injury report index.

The availability-drag metric, and why it is not a projection

drag(team) = sum over that team's listed players of weight(status) weights: Out 1.00 · Doubtful 0.75 · Questionable 0.50 · Probable 0.25 · Available 0.00 G-League assignments and two-way listings are counted separately and excluded.

This is an ordinal exposure score, not points and not minutes. Its only job is to sort games by how much uncertainty the roster carries, so the pick screening can throw out signals built on a roster we know is wrong. Calling it anything more specific would be inventing precision the source does not contain.

Note that it is unitless and unbounded. A team with four Out players and a team with four Out players plus a Doubtful are ordered correctly; neither number should be read as "points lost".

Why this is not "the team loses X points". A replacement absorbs part of an absent starter's production, usage redistributes to teammates, and the opponent's defensive attention changes. Converting "player averages 21.8" into "team loses 21.8" is the single most common analytical error in injury coverage. This build reports the player's own number and refuses to make that leap.

Game flow — what the model does and does not see

Modelled

  • Expected possessions per game, from both teams' pace. Published on every row of Originated lines: it is what converts a per-100-possession rating into a points number.
  • Margin distribution width, measured at 17.15 points. This is what makes a cover probability a probability rather than a guess, and it is the term that makes close games genuinely uncertain.
  • Blowout asymmetry is captured only through that width. A 15-point spread has a much wider plausible outcome set than a 2-point spread, and probabilities reflect it.

Not modelled

  • Rotation minutes — no minutes source was retrieved. This is the binding constraint on the entire prop programme.
  • In-game score-state effects — garbage-time minutes, foul trouble, late-game fouling. No play-by-play or lineup data was retrieved.
  • Rest and travel — back-to-backs and travel legs are not in the rating. The schedule is known, so this is a fixable gap rather than an architectural one.
  • Coach and scheme changes — not captured anywhere in this build.

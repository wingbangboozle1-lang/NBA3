Why so many rules have no sample

Three reasons, and none of them is fixed by lowering a threshold after seeing the result:

  • No player data. The three prop rules need per-player game logs for a season. Only two teams' top-8 minutes were captured and no logs exist, so the rules are registered and BLOCKED, not quietly deleted.
  • The signal never fires. The disagreement rules (S11, S12) need the estimators to differ by more than their combined error. Across 131 walk-forward games they never did — the two methods agree far more often than that, which is itself a finding about the models.
  • Cross-game dependence is unmeasurable here, so the two-game parlay has no price and cannot be graded.

The null control did its job. N01 is a coin flip. It returned a hit rate indistinguishable from chance, as it must. If it had shown an edge, the measurement would have been broken rather than the market.

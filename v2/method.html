Method

Method

How the pipeline works, and what is broken.

Two independent estimators, disagreement published as the headline, blend underneath. This page says how each piece works and, where a piece does not work, that it does not.

Estimator A — result-based

Ridge least squares on final margins, one team strength per team, with the home-court term subtracted before the fit so it cannot be re-estimated inside it. Every forecast is made from games dated strictly before the game being priced, and the home-court pin is recomputed from history at each date too, so neither the ratings nor the pin see the future.

Estimator B — efficiency-based

Possessions times points per possession, from the official team advanced and opponent pages. Never looks at a final margin, which is what makes it a genuinely different method rather than a re-parameterisation of A.

points per possession  = (points per game x games) / possessions
expected possessions   = (pace_home + pace_away) / 2
total                  = possessions x (ppp_home + ppp_away)
margin                 = possessions x (ppp_home - ppp_away) + hca_points

The total is built that way on purpose. A total computed as the average of two team scoring averages double-counts pace and cannot be corrected afterwards; the possession form at least has the right units. It still does not beat a constant league total out of sample, and the site says so on the page where the totals appear.

Disagreement

The headline number is the gap between the estimators in points, and beside it the same gap divided by their combined error. Three points of disagreement when each method is typically wrong by fifteen is nothing; three points when each is wrong by four is a real fight. Only the second statement is worth acting on, so the label is computed from the ratio and the raw gap is never shown alone.

Gates

Eleven gates per rule: provenance, look-ahead, sample, power, exact-binomial significance, Benjamini-Hochberg control across the board, half-to-half stability, economics against the price actually paid, the board-wide luck threshold, pre-registration, and whether a real market price exists. The last one fails for everything, which is the honest state of a repository with no odds feed.

Ledger

Each pick is hashed with SHA-256 over its canonical JSON before settlement; the hash is stored and recomputed after. The strategy's own registration — signal, thresholds, universe, settlement rule — is hashed the same way and frozen in data/v2/registrations.json on the first build, so a rule cannot be re-stamped forward to stay ahead of a bet.

Modules

__init__.py, backtest.py, build.py, estimators.py, gates.py, injury.py, leaderboard.py, pricing.py, schedule_context.py, stats.py, strategies.py

Reproducibility

The build is seeded and byte-identical across runs; a test asserts it. Two things had to be pinned for that to be true — the publication timestamp to the minute, and the registration stamps to a file written once.

Known broken

  • The totals model does not beat a constant. Published as worse, not shrunk into looking fine.
  • Estimator B's backtest is contaminated by its single dated snapshot (FV2-06).
  • Player props and four of the nine correlation cells need per-player game logs that do not exist in this repository.
  • The travel term is a great-circle lower bound from a secondary source, not route mileage.
  • No market price, so no rule can be shown to have value.

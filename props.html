Why props are the hardest market to originate honestly

  • Minutes are the number. A player averaging None points can clear almost any line or none, depending on whether he plays 17 or 34 minutes. Blocks, steals and rebounds scale even more steeply.
  • Blowout risk is asymmetric. A 15-point game can delete a fourth quarter for the starter you picked and hand it to a bench player you did not.
  • Availability resolves late. The official report is a snapshot that keeps being revised up to tip-off, so a prop priced at 5 p.m. can be wrong at 7 p.m. with no change in the number.
Consequence for this build. Player prop combos (strategy S6) are the only strategy with zero validation gates passed. It is blocked, not merely unproven.

Correlation — measured, with error bars

Parlays live or die on dependence. Multiplying leg probabilities assumes independence, and that assumption is exactly what a same-game parlay violates. Below is what was actually measured on 86 completed games, and — just as importantly — what could not be measured at all.

What the numbers say

  • Away total vs home total: r = +0.082. Both totals share the same possession count, but the interval includes zero, so with this sample the dependence is weak and not established. Do not build a parlay model on it yet.
  • Margin vs total: r = +0.005, interval spanning zero. Spread and total legs in the same game can be multiplied as approximately independent without much error — which is the useful, boring answer.
  • Sample honesty: n = 86 is enough to rule out a large correlation and not enough to establish a small one. The intervals show that explicitly rather than hiding it behind a point estimate.

What could not be measured

These are the correlations that actually matter for the prop combos the brief asks about. Each is blank, not estimated:

  • Points vs rebounds for the same player
  • A player's points vs a teammate's points
  • A player's over vs the team total over
  • Minutes vs any prop, and therefore blowout-conditioned props

All four need per-player game logs, which were not collected. Every one is listed on Flagged for review with what would be required to close it.

Prop market inventory

A single game exposes 1,170 individual prop price points across 47 pages of the upstream feed. Athlete names come back as opaque numeric ids, so resolving every one is a lookup per player. Five were resolved and are shown above with their real names and club, cross-checked against each player's own record. The rest are deliberately not published, because a price without a correctly identified player is worse than no price.

Originated Lines

Our own spreads and totals, originated from official NBA efficiency data — no sportsbook prices used.

How these lines are built

The published line is the model that survived the backtest. It is the LM-C blend: the average of an additive points-for/points-against estimate and a Massey least-squares margin estimate, both fitted on all 174 verified results. Out-of-sample it reduced margin RMSE from 17.30 to 16.18 points, which is 13% of the variance in game margins — a real but modest edge over guessing the league average, and the honest ceiling of what this data supports.

margin = ½ × (PPGhome + PAPGaway − PPGaway − PAPGhome) + ½ × (HCA + rhome − raway)

The total is the sum of the two teams' own scoring rates (the T2 estimator, which beat the alternative T1 estimator out-of-sample). Both are originated lines — ours, not copied from any sportsbook. No market price enters the calculation anywhere.

The efficiency estimate is shown only as a cross-check. It uses the official season OffRtg / DefRtg / Pace table: E[home pts] = PACE × (OffRtghome + DefRtgaway − OffRtgleague) / 100. It is a better-specified model in principle, but we hold only season-to-date ratings, so putting it through the same walk-forward backtest would leak future games. It is therefore published as a second opinion, clearly labelled, and not used as the line of record. Where the two disagree by more than 6 points, the row is tagged.

The ± columns are 1.96 × the out-of-sample residual standard deviation: 16.2 points on the spread and 20.3 points on the total. Those bands are very wide, and that is the honest picture — a single NBA game has roughly 16 points of margin noise that no model removes, which is exactly why none of the strategies on the leaderboard can clear its sample-size gate yet. The home-win probability assumes the margin is normal around the originated spread with the margin model's residual standard deviation (16.18 points); that normality assumption is unverified and is listed as such on the flagged page.

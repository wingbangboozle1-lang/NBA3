Rest, back-to-backs and the official final scores

Does days-of-rest carry a measurable signal in the official results, or is it noise? The site lists rest as a game-flow factor (GF04) but records the effect as unmeasured. These numbers measure it.

Days of rest = the gap in days between a team's game and its previous game, derived from the official game dates in the 183-game log. rest_diff = home rest minus away rest. Correlations are Pearson with a Fisher z 95% interval; the p-value for rest_diff vs home margin comes from 5,000 permutations of the margins (seed 20261114), which assumes nothing about the distribution.

These are observational counts of one 25-day slice of one season, not a controlled experiment. Rest is confounded with travel, opponent quality and coaching. A small n in the back-to-back cells is exactly why the intervals are published next to the point estimates.

Do our errors cluster inside one slate? (cross-game parlay dependence)

Cross-game parlays are priced as if the legs are independent. That is an assumption, and the site says so. If residuals on the same night move together, a cross-game parlay of our own picks is mispriced in a known direction.

For every pair of games that share a date, correlate their out-of-sample residuals (both orderings, so the estimate is symmetric). The null shuffles residuals across dates 5,000 times (seed 20261114+1), which destroys any within-night structure while keeping the same residuals and the same slate sizes. p is the fraction of shuffled values at least as extreme as observed. The second test asks whether the per-date side hit rate varies more than a coin with a constant rate would (5,000 shuffles, seed 20261114+2).
Cover convention. The home side covers our originated spread when act_margin > pred_margin, the same convention the site uses in scripts/strategy_lab.py::grade_game.

A p near 1 means the per-night hit rates vary less than chance, i.e. no detectable slate effect. A p near 0 means our errors share a nightly component, which is the thing that would break independent parlay pricing.
140 games over roughly 30 dates is a small sample for a variance-component question, and the 1,176 pairs are not independent: one game on a ten-game night contributes eighteen pairs. The Fisher-z interval above therefore assumes something that is not true and is printed only for completeness - the permutation p, which reshuffles whole slates and keeps the dependence structure, is the number to read. The honest output here is 'no detectable slate effect', not a verdict of independence.

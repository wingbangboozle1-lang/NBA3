Strategies and the leaderboard

Six strategies, each scored against the same seven validation gates. The leaderboard is deliberately unfriendly: it scores only what has been measured, and then discounts that score by how easily it could have arisen from luck.

The scoring rule, in one line. A strategy's entry is (improvement in Brier score over a naive baseline) x (1 - p), where p is the p-value of the luck test on that strategy's observed-versus-expected results. A run that is indistinguishable from chance scores near zero however good it looks. This is the mechanism that removes luck from the table.

Why only one strategy has a score

Five of the six strategies need historical prices to be scored, and no closing-line archive was retrieved. Without prices you can compute a probability but you cannot compute a return, so those five carry no score at all rather than a fabricated one.

This is the single biggest gap in the build. Closing it is suggestion 1.

How much sample a real edge would need

Using a two-sided test at 95% confidence and 80% power, detecting a genuine edge with a coin-flip market requires roughly:

Edge sizeBets requiredBasis
A 5% edge≈ 778 bets own computation derived
A 3% edge≈ 2159 bets own computation derived

A 23-game slate therefore cannot demonstrate anything. Any leaderboard shorter than a few hundred settled bets is measuring variance, and saying so is the point of the luck adjustment.

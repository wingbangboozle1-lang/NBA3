Correlations

Measured correlation values for parlay construction — and an explicit list of the ones we could not measure.

Measured correlations

Correlation is what decides whether a same-game parlay is fairly priced. If two legs are positively correlated, the joint probability is higher than the product of the legs, and a book that prices the parlay as independent is giving value away — and vice versa. We measure what we can and refuse to invent the rest.

Working the arithmetic: under independence the two legs would both land 21.9% of the time (50.9% × 43.0%). In the 114 out-of-sample games both legs actually landed 24.6% of the time. The gap of +2.7% points in the direction of positive correlation, but the residual correlation itself (r=+0.091) has a 95% confidence interval that contains zero, so this is suggestive rather than established. It is exactly the kind of number that needs 300+ games before anyone should size a position on it.

What we cannot tell you. Player-prop correlations — the points-points and points-rebounds pairs that drive most same-game parlays — are blank above. They require per-player game logs, which were not captured in this session. We left them blank and flagged them rather than filling in plausible numbers, because a fabricated correlation is worse than no correlation: it gets priced into parlays.

Cross-game parlays

Independence between games is the standard assumption, and it is the one nobody checks. We have not checked it either — the sample of 114 games does not support a reliable estimate of the correlation between two different games played on the same night. Treat the 0.0 above as an assumption awaiting evidence, not a measurement.

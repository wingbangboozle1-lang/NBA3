1. Same-game two-leggers

The dependence ratio below is measured, not assumed: it is the observed joint hit rate of “our side covers and the game goes over our total” divided by what that rate would be if the two legs were independent. A ratio above 1.0 means the legs land together more often than independence pricing assumes — which is precisely how a sportsbook protects itself on same-game parlays.

2. Cross-game two-leggers

Independence across games on the same night is an assumption, not a measurement. It was not tested on this sample — two games tipping off at the same time share no players, but they do share a travel schedule, a referee pool and a news cycle, none of which this repository can measure. The fair prices below are correct only if that assumption holds.

4. What a same-game parlay actually costs you

A two-legger priced as independent at 50% × 50% pays +300 for a 25% chance — a fair bet. If the legs are positively correlated, the true joint probability is higher than 25% and the +300 price is now generous; if they are negatively correlated, as our sample slightly suggests, the true probability is lower and the same price is a tax. The size of the tax is the dependence ratio above, and the interval on it includes 1.0, so on this sample we cannot tell which way it goes. That is the honest answer, and it is why nothing here is funded.

Why C1 and C2 are run separately. Both end in the number 183, and that is the point: one route counts team-games off the official statistics page, the other counts games off the scoreboard calendar and adds our own October rows. Two independent official readings agreeing is what lets the extended log be called verified rather than merely transcribed. The rebuild fails if either route disagrees.

C7 and C8 exist because two sessions did the same refresh by accident. On 2026-11-14, about 40 minutes apart, this session and session bd90ce16 each appended the nine 2026-11-13 finals and each transcribed the injury report, into different files, from the same official pages. Each then became the engine's input by a different route — and the collision is what turned the Pages build red on main for one cycle (flag NF-66). The reconciliation is not a tie-break: the canonical log stays the input, the other capture is compared against it on every build, and both agreements are published here instead of being mentioned in a commit message. The injury comparison is not a clean pass: the one difference is the Traore team cell (check C8, flag NF-59), and whether to adopt the other read's attribution and move to the newer stamp is CQ-47.

6. Report availability, game by game

The rule, verbatim from the official index: “By 5 p.m. local time the day before a game (other than the second day of a back-to-back), teams must designate a participation status and identify a specific injury, illness, other medical condition, or other reason for any player whose participation in the game may be affected for any reason. For the second game of a back-to-back, teams must report the above information by 1 p.m. local time on the day of the game. In addition, teams are required to submit a game-day injury report between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. local time (and between 8 and 10 a.m. for tip-offs 5 p.m. or earlier) on game days (besides the second game of a back-to-back).” — official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2026-27-season/.

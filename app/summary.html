1. What this project is, in one paragraph

A research pipeline that originates its own NBA lines from official results, tests them out of sample against nine validation gates, ranks the resulting strategies on a leaderboard with an explicit luck threshold, and publishes every intermediate number next to the official source it came from. There is no market price anywhere in this repository: no licensed odds feed is connected. Every “pick” on this site is a fair price against our own line, which makes its expected value zero by construction. The backtest therefore does not look for profit — it looks for bias in our own line, which is the only question this data can answer.

2. The headline result, stated plainly

  • Zero strategies are funded. Nothing on the board passes all nine gates.
  • Nothing beats luck. Simulating the whole board under the fair-line null, the best-looking rule clears 28.9 percentage points of edge 5% of the time. The largest edge any real rule posted is 10.7 pp.
  • The margin model is honest but weak. Out-of-sample RMSE 15.94 against a 16.92 constant baseline, and it is unbiased (0.81 points).
  • The totals model is broken. Out-of-sample RMSE 19.45 is worse than the 19.39 constant baseline, and it runs 1.5 points hot. It is kept only because the brief asks for originated totals, and it is shrunk toward the league mean. See NF-49.
  • An earlier “signal” was the model’s error. Before regularisation, “fade the highest projected totals” posted 69.7% on 66 games (p=0.0019). After regularisation it fell to 65.5% on 29 games (p=0.136). The signal was our own miscalibration. See NF-50.

4. The engine, in four sentences

A Massey least-squares margin model fits one rating per team plus a home-court term on all completed games. A totals model regresses the game total on the two teams’ prior scoring environment. Both are shrunk — a ridge penalty on the ratings and a blend weight pulling the totals toward the league mean — and both shrinkage parameters are chosen by validating on the most recent 20% of past games only, never on the games being forecast. Every date is then priced with the engine fitted on strictly earlier dates, so nothing downstream has seen the future.

5. How luck is removed

Three separate mechanisms, because one is not enough:

  • Exact binomial tests against the fair-line rate for the market — 50% for a single bet, 25% for a two-leg parlay. This is gate G5.
  • Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across every scored rule on the board, because a board of 15 rules will throw up one “significant” result by chance. This is gate G6.
  • A whole-board Monte Carlo: every rule is simulated under its own fair-line null, the largest edge anywhere on the board is recorded, and that is repeated 5,000 times. The 95th percentile — 28.9 pp — is the edge a rule must beat before it is called anything. This is gate G9, and nothing on the board beats it.

On top of that, rules are published in mirror pairs (back a big home favourite / fade it). They select the same games and are exact complements, so they are one finding, not two. And a null control — back the home team in every game — is scored alongside them; it fails every gate, which is the check that the gates themselves work.

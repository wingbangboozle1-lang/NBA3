Overview

Method & rules

1. What is originated here

Two numbers per game: a point spread, quoted for the home team the way a book prints it, and a total. Both come from a walk-forward engine whose inputs are official: the official schedule, official team advanced statistics, official final scores and the official injury report. Nothing is copied from a sportsbook, because no sportsbook data is connected to this repository.

The engine is scripts/strategy_lab.py, standard library only, seeded and deterministic — two consecutive runs produce byte-identical output, which is what lets the CI workflow detect drift.

2. The model

  • Margin. A Massey least-squares model: one rating per team plus a home-court term, fitted on completed games. The prediction for a game is rating(home) − rating(away) + home-court.
  • Total. An ordinary least-squares regression of the game total on the two teams' prior scoring environment (points for and against, both teams).
  • Shrinkage, chosen from the past. A ridge penalty on the team ratings and a blend weight pulling the totals toward the league mean are both selected by validating on the most recent 20% of past games. This matters: before shrinkage the ratings of teams on a bad run exploded and the engine printed a 28-point spread, which no real line does.
  • No look-ahead. For every date, the engine is refitted from games with a strictly earlier date. Games are batched by date, so a game cannot see another game tipped off the same night — a leak an earlier session's pipeline actually had.

The Brier score is in-sample on the walk-forward residuals. The residual distribution that turns a margin into a probability is estimated from the same forecasts it scores. That flatters the number, it is not a calibration test on held-out data, and it is labelled as such wherever it appears.

4. Removing luck from the evaluation

Luck cannot be removed from a single game. It can be kept out of the score, and this project uses four mechanisms, because one is not enough:

  1. Exact binomial tests against the fair-line rate for the market — 50% for a single bet, 25% for a two-leg parlay. Not a normal approximation: an exact test. (Gate G5.)
  2. Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control across every scored rule on the board. Fifteen rules at p < 0.05 produce a false positive more often than not. (Gate G6.)
  3. A whole-board Monte Carlo. Every rule is simulated under its own fair-line null, the largest edge anywhere on the board is recorded, and that is repeated 5,000 times. The 95th percentile — 28.9 percentage points — is the edge a rule must beat before it is called anything. This is the test that catches the fact that the best rule on a large board is biased upward even after FDR correction. (Gate G9.) Nothing on the board beats it.
  4. Date-block bootstrap for the return interval, because betting results cluster by date — same slate, same travel, same news. The resampling unit is the date, not the bet. (Gate G8.)

Three further disciplines are structural rather than statistical. Rules are published in mirror pairs so the losing direction cannot be hidden. Strategy thresholds are rolling quantiles of prior projections rather than fixed numbers, so a rule cannot die or appear just because the model changed. And a null control — back the home team every game — is scored alongside everything else; it fails 5 of 9 gates, which is the check that the gates work.

The most important methodological point on this site. Because our line is originated to be fair, the expected value of betting into it is zero by construction. A rule that wins more than half the time against our own line is therefore evidence that our line is biased in the games the rule picks — a model defect to repair, not an edge to bet. This project reports those biases as defects (see CQ-40). The one apparent exception found during this build — "fade the highest projected totals", which posted 69.7% on 66 games before the totals model was regularised — fell to 65.5% on 29 games once the model was fixed. The signal was the model's error.

5. Injuries and rotation

The official injury report publishes a participation status and a reason. It does not publish minutes, expected minutes, or production impact, and a status is not a medical clearance. This project therefore:

  • captures the newest report with its timestamp and keeps the previous snapshot so status changes can be shown;
  • treats a team printed NOT YET SUBMITTED as unknown, never as healthy — "unknown" and "fit" are different facts and every downstream number respects the difference;
  • sums the official season minutes, and the official season points, rebounds and assists, of players listed Out or Doubtful — and only those, because anything softer is a guess;
  • publishes that vacuum as a floor and counts how many listed players fall outside the captured minutes table, contributing zero;
  • leaves the replacement blank. Who absorbs the vacated minutes, and how much of the production they replace, needs per-player game logs and on/off splits that this repository does not have. No availability weights (Out 0.00 / Doubtful 0.25 / Questionable 0.60 / Probable 0.90 / Available 1.00) are applied anywhere in the current pipeline; earlier sessions used such weights and they are recorded here only as history.

6. Legal and data-use boundary

  • The NBA Terms of Use restrict the use of NBA Statistics in connection with gambling and restrict reproduction. This is a research repository, publishes no market prices and no betting advice, and links to every official source it uses. The review points at nba.com/termsofuse are listed on the Flagged for review page.
  • Publishing betting-related functionality or recommendations in the Netherlands requires a licence under the Betting and Gaming Act and a Ksa licence for online sports-betting offers; the statutory position is summarised on the Open questions page.
  • No licensed price feed is connected, so no profit, ROI or market comparison can be published here. Every price shown is our own fair price, and every ROI figure is measured against our own line at an assumed −110 — which is a property of our numbers, not an economic result (NF-55).

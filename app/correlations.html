Why the same-game number matters

The joint hit rate of “our side covers and the game goes over our total” was 22.14% against 23.51% under independence — a dependence ratio of 0.942. On this sample the ratio sits slightly below 1.0, meaning the two legs landed together a little less often than independence would suggest. The confidence interval on the underlying residual correlation includes zero, so independence is not rejected — we cannot tell whether the true value is above or below 1.0. That is the honest position, and it is why the same-game cards on the parlays page are published with the interval attached rather than as clean prices.

Why the player numbers are blank

Every player-level correlation needs per-player game logs: one row per player per game, roughly 4,000 rows a season. This repository has never captured them — it holds official season averages for 31 players and nothing else. Without the game-by-game distribution there is no honest way to put a number in those cells, so they stay empty and are listed on the Flagged for review page. Four prop combinations are blocked on exactly this gap.

The cross-game assumption

Two legs in different games are priced as independent on the parlays page. That is an assumption, printed as one. Testing it properly needs matched same-night results with a shared context model, which this sample is too small to support.
